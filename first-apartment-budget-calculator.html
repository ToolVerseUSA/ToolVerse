Why there are two affordability tests

A person can have enough monthly income for a rent payment but not enough cash for the deposit, first month, moving, and setup costs. The reverse can also happen: enough savings to move but a monthly budget that leaves no room for food, transportation, debt, or a buffer. Separating both tests makes the trade-off visible.

Methodology and limitations

ToolVerse uses the shared 2026-planning-v1 method, last reviewed August 22, 2026, to estimate take-home pay from income, filing status, and state. Federal and payroll estimates are simplified; state income tax uses planning rates. All apartment, utility, insurance, transportation, debt, grocery, and move-in figures are entered by you. Actual tax, leasing, deposit, utility, roommate, and household circumstances can differ.

Plan your next step

If you are still deciding what rent to target, use the Salary Needed for Rent Calculator. For an ongoing scenario with a full Financial Score, use the core affordability calculator. The 30% Rent Rule is a starting reference, not a complete apartment budget.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between move-in costs and monthly apartment costs?

Move-in costs are one-time cash needs such as a deposit, fees, the first month's rent, moving, and setup costs. Monthly costs repeat and can include rent, utilities, insurance, transportation, debt, food, and savings goals.

Does this calculator assume a standard security deposit or utility bill?

No. ToolVerse does not invent those amounts. Enter the property-specific costs you know, and leave an unknown category at zero until you can confirm it.

Does a positive result mean I will be approved for an apartment?

No. Landlord screening, income requirements, credit checks, lease terms, guarantors, and local rules vary. This is an educational cash-flow estimate, not a lease-approval tool.
