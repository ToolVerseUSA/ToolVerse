Scenario context

These are arithmetic equivalents, not a market forecast. Overtime, unpaid leave, bonuses, benefits, deductions, and variable schedules can change actual pay.

What this calculator can—and cannot—tell you

It can help you compare an offer expressed as annual salary with a monthly or hourly view. It cannot determine whether an employer is paying fairly, whether a credential will raise pay, or what a specific job will offer.

Billing and coding work can overlap with health information and revenue-cycle operations, but job titles differ across employers. A “medical coder” or “medical biller” posting may include different responsibilities, specialty knowledge, productivity expectations, and credential preferences.

The BLS benchmark below covers the broader medical records specialist occupation. It is useful context, but it is not a direct salary promise for this role.

BLS broad benchmark: $51,140 annual median wage and $24.59 hourly median wage for medical records specialists in May 2025. Use your own offer or local evidence for a role-specific decision.

Sources and limitations

ToolVerse uses public sources for context, not a promise of an individual wage, job, reimbursement result, or vendor outcome.

  • U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — Medical Records SpecialistsBLS reports a national median wage of $51,140 per year and $24.59 per hour in May 2025 for the broader medical records specialist occupation. That category is a reference point, not a direct wage for every biller or coder.
  • CMS — Healthcare Common Procedure Coding SystemCMS explains the relationship between CPT as HCPCS Level I and CMS-maintained HCPCS Level II.
  • CMS — Electronic Health Care ClaimsCMS describes electronic submission, front-end edits, rejections, acknowledgments, and claim denials.
  • HHS — Business AssociatesHHS identifies billing and claims processing as examples of services that may involve protected health information and business-associate obligations.
