What this decision involves

Debt decisions require more than a lower monthly payment. This guide explains how to compare a current-payment baseline, a consolidation loan, and a debt-settlement scenario without treating any outcome as guaranteed.

How to use the calculator

Start with the total balance and minimum payments. Then compare the proposed consolidation principal after fees, the scheduled payment, total repayment, and expected interest. A settlement scenario should separately show the assumed settlement percentage, program fee, and any modeled delay.

Important limitations

Settlement is a different risk category, not simply a cheaper loan. Creditors may refuse, fees may be substantial, delinquency can damage credit, and canceled debt may create tax questions. Use the calculator for scenario analysis and confirm details with official consumer-protection resources.

Frequently asked questions

How is debt consolidation different from settlement?

Consolidation replaces several debts with one new loan or balance. Settlement seeks creditor agreement to accept less than owed, often with different fees and credit consequences.

Does debt settlement guarantee savings?

No. Creditors may refuse, fees vary, and any modeled result is only a scenario.

Can settlement affect my credit or taxes?

Delinquency may damage credit and canceled debt can have tax consequences. Review official guidance and qualified advice.
