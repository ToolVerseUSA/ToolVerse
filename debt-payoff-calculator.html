Debt Payoff Calculator: Snowball vs Avalanche

Enter your balances, APRs, and minimum payments once — then compare a snowball plan against an avalanche plan month by month, with payoff dates and total interest for each.

Planning information only. Payoff dates and interest totals are planning estimates from the balances, APRs, and payments you enter — not financial advice, tax advice, or a guarantee of any result. Creditors calculate interest and post payments in different ways, so real outcomes will differ.

Compare snowball vs avalanche with your debts

Add each debt with its balance, APR, and minimum payment. Everything runs in your browser — no account, nothing uploaded.

How the two methods work

Both methods pay the minimum on every debt and aim every spare dollar at one target debt. They only differ in how the target is chosen. The snowball method targets the smallest balance first, so early wins arrive quickly and freed payments roll into the next debt. The avalanche method targets the highest APR first, which usually lowers total interest because the most expensive balance shrinks first.

Why the roll-over matters

When a debt clears, its minimum payment does not disappear from your budget — it rolls into the next target. That growing payment is what accelerates the later, larger debts in both methods.

QuestionSnowballAvalanche
First targetSmallest balanceHighest APR
Typical strengthFast early wins and momentumLowest total interest in most cases
Typical trade-offMay cost more interest overallFirst payoff can take longer to reach
Same for bothMinimums on all debts, extra payments, and the roll-over effect

When a debt never pays off

If a minimum payment is at or below the monthly interest on a balance, that balance cannot shrink on minimums alone. This calculator caps its simulation at 50 years (600 months) and warns you instead of showing a misleading date. Raising the extra payment, or aiming extra at that debt first, is what changes the outcome.

Frequently asked questions

Is snowball or avalanche better?
Avalanche usually costs less total interest because it removes the highest-APR balance first. Snowball clears small debts sooner, which many people find motivating. The better method is the one you will keep following every month.
What if I can only pay the minimums?
Enter $0 as the extra payment. The calculator still compares both methods, but if a minimum is at or below the monthly interest on a balance, that balance will not shrink — the tool warns you rather than inventing a payoff date.
Do balance transfers change the math?
Yes. A transfer can move a balance to a lower APR for a promotional window, which changes which debt an avalanche targets first. Enter the post-transfer APR and balance to model it, and remember transfer fees and the post-promotion rate are not included here.
Why do both methods finish in the same month so often?
When debts have similar balances and APRs, the two orderings behave almost identically. The interest difference grows when one debt is much more expensive than the others.
