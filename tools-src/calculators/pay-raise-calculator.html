About the math: if you enter a raise percent, new pay is current pay times 1 plus the percent divided by 100. If you enter a new pay amount instead, the percent change is the new pay minus the current pay, divided by the current pay, times 100. Conversions assume a 40 hour week and 52 weeks a year: weekly pay is hourly pay times 40, annual pay is weekly pay times 52, and monthly pay is annual pay divided by 12.

Worked example: at $25 per hour with a 10 percent raise, the new rate is $27.50 per hour, $1,100 per week, and $57,200 per year, up from $52,000 per year, a raise of $5,200 per year. Going the other way, $25 to $27.50 is a 10 percent raise.
