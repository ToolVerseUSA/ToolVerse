Should you choose Roth or traditional?

Traditional contributions go in pre-tax and are taxed when you withdraw them. Roth contributions go in after tax and come out tax-free. Enter your contribution, your tax rate now, your expected rate in retirement, your years to retirement, and your growth rate to see which leaves you with more after tax.

Worked example: contributing $7,500 a year for 30 years at 7% growth, with a 22% rate now and a 12% rate in retirement. The annuity factor is about 94.46. The traditional account grows to about $708,456 before tax and about $623,441 after the 12% retirement tax. The Roth account invests $5,850 a year after the 22% tax and grows to about $552,596 tax-free. Traditional wins by about $70,846.

The rule of thumb: Roth usually wins when your retirement rate will be higher than your rate today, because you pay tax now at the lower rate. Traditional usually wins when your retirement rate will be lower, because you deduct at today's higher rate and pay later at the lower rate. If the rates are the same, the two come out equal before other rules are considered.

About the math: the future value of annual contributions is FV = C x (((1 + r)^n - 1) / r), with contributions at the end of each year. The traditional account invests the full contribution and is taxed at the retirement rate on withdrawal. The Roth account invests the contribution minus the current tax and is tax-free at withdrawal.
