Methodology and sources

How this estimate is calculated

Federal income tax uses progressive 2026 brackets and standard deductions by filing status. Payroll taxes include employee Social Security, Medicare, and the additional Medicare threshold where applicable.

State income tax uses simplified state planning rates for all states and Washington, D.C. Actual state liability can differ because of brackets, deductions, credits, local taxes, and residency rules.

Housing, debt, cash flow, savings capacity, and essential-expense pressure contribute to the Financial Score. The result is a decision aid, not certified tax advice.

Method references: IRS Form 1040 guidance, SSA 2026 wage base, and IRS 2026 inflation adjustments.

Frequently asked questions

A practical US affordability check

How much rent can I afford?

A common starting point is 25%–30% of estimated take-home pay, but debt, childcare, insurance, transportation, and savings goals can make a lower amount more comfortable.

What percentage should go to housing?

The calculator flags housing pressure around the 30% take-home range, then looks at debt and remaining cash rather than relying on one rule alone.

How is the Financial Score calculated?

It blends housing burden, debt burden, monthly cash flow, savings capacity, and essential-expense pressure. Each category is shown so the score is explainable.

How does estimated take-home pay work?

Gross income is converted to annual income, then estimated federal income tax, state income tax, Social Security, and Medicare are subtracted. Benefits and deductions are not modeled.

What is the 50/30/20 rule?

It is a budgeting framework that suggests 50% for needs, 30% for wants, and 20% for savings or debt. Your actual budget may reasonably differ.

Is $2,000 rent affordable on $70,000?

It depends on state, filing status, debt, and other costs. Enter those details instead of relying on salary alone.

Is this calculator free?

Yes. There is no login or email requirement.

Is my financial information stored?

Inputs stay in your browser. Recent history is optional Local Storage data that you can clear at any time.

Is this a tax calculator?

No. It is a simplified educational estimate, not tax preparation or a filing result.

Does it support all US states?

Yes. The selector includes all 50 states plus Washington, D.C., with simplified planning rates.

Can I use it without an account?

Yes. The calculator is designed to work without signup.

Does it work on mobile?

Yes. Inputs use mobile-friendly numeric keyboards and the layout collapses into a single column.
