Questions people ask

What is the 25x rule for retirement?

The 25x rule says you need retirement savings equal to 25 times your annual spending in retirement. It comes from the 4% rule: if you withdraw 4% of your savings in the first year of retirement and adjust for inflation after that, your money has historically lasted about 30 years. So if you want to spend $50,000 a year, your target is $1,250,000. It is a rule of thumb, not a guarantee.

How do I know if I am saving enough for retirement?

Compare your projected balance at retirement with your 25x target. If you are short, this calculator shows the monthly saving needed to close the gap. Saving more, retiring later, or planning to spend less in retirement all shrink the gap.

Does this include Social Security?

No. This calculator looks only at your own savings and contributions. Social Security is separate income on top of your savings — use the Social Security benefit estimator to see what that could add.

Are these numbers guaranteed?

Yes — every number here is an estimate. The projection assumes a steady annual return with monthly compounding and does not adjust for inflation, taxes, fees, or market ups and downs. Real results will differ.
