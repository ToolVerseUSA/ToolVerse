When NOT to use Student Loan Payoff Calculator

  • This is an estimate only — your servicer may charge fees, apply payments differently, or offer income-driven or forgiveness plans this tool does not model.
  • It assumes a fixed interest rate and the same payment every month; variable rates and changing payments change the result.
  • The avalanche vs snowball comparison assumes each loan's monthly interest is covered first and the rest attacks one loan — real minimum payments may differ.

Questions people ask

How does the student loan payoff calculator work?

Enter your loan balance, annual interest rate and monthly payment. The calculator uses standard amortization math to work out how many months it takes to pay the loan off, how much interest you pay in total, and your payoff date.

Can I see how extra payments help?

Yes. Enter an extra monthly payment and the calculator compares your standard payment with the standard payment plus the extra amount, showing the months and interest you save.

What is the difference between avalanche and snowball?

The avalanche method attacks the highest-interest loan first and usually costs the least in total interest. The snowball method attacks the smallest balance first, which can feel more motivating because a loan disappears sooner. Enter a second loan and a total monthly payment to compare both.

Is this my exact payoff date?

No. This is an estimate only, based on the numbers you enter. Your loan servicer may charge fees, apply payments differently, or offer income-driven plans. Always confirm your balance and rate with your servicer.
