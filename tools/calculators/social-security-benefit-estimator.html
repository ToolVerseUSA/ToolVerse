When NOT to use Social Security Benefit Estimator

  • This is an estimate, not your official SSA benefit — your real benefit depends on your full 35-year earnings record, which this tool does not use.
  • The break-even totals ignore future COLAs, taxes on benefits, and earnings-test reductions if you work before full retirement age.
  • If you already claimed or receive spousal or survivor benefits, the standard reduction and delayed credit rules here may not apply to you.

Questions people ask

What is my PIA (Primary Insurance Amount)?

Your Primary Insurance Amount (PIA) is the monthly benefit you get if you claim at your full retirement age. Find it on your official statement at ssa.gov, or enter your current benefit and the age it applies to and this tool works your PIA out from it.

Should I claim Social Security at 62, 67 or 70?

Claiming at 62 with a full retirement age of 67 cuts the monthly benefit by 30%. Waiting until 70 adds 24% on top of the full amount. The trade-off is time: claiming early means more years of payments at a lower amount, so the break-even table below shows total amounts received by ages 80 and 85.

What is the 2027 Social Security COLA?

The Social Security Administration announced a 3.6% cost-of-living adjustment (COLA) for 2027 on October 14, 2026. The average retired worker gets about $75 more per month. This tool uses 3.6% as the default assumption, and you can change it.

Is this my official Social Security benefit?

No. This is an estimate based on the standard SSA reduction and delayed credit rules. Your official benefit depends on your full earnings record. Get your official statement at ssa.gov.
