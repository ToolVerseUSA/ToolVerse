About the math: the gain needed equals the loss divided by (1 minus the loss). A 20% loss needs a 25% gain, and a 50% loss needs a 100% gain. Years to recover use the compound growth formula at the steady return you enter.

When NOT to use Drawdown Recovery Calculator

  • The recovery time assumes the same return every year. Real returns vary and can be negative.
  • It shows the gain needed on the remaining balance alone, with no new contributions.
  • The result is a math illustration, not investment advice or a forecast.

Questions people ask

Why does a 20% loss need a 25% gain to recover?

Because the gain applies to a smaller balance. A $10,000 portfolio that falls 20% is worth $8,000. It needs $2,000 to get back to $10,000, and $2,000 is 25% of $8,000. The formula is loss divided by (1 minus loss).

How long will recovery take?

It depends on the return you assume. At a steady 7% a year, a 25% gain takes about 3.3 years. Enter any return to see the estimate, but real returns vary every year, so the time is an illustration, not a promise.

Does this include new contributions?

No. This is the gain needed on the remaining balance alone. Adding new money would shorten the recovery, but this tool shows the pure math of the loss.

Is the recovery return a forecast?

No. It is a steady rate you enter for illustration. Investment returns are never steady, and past returns do not guarantee future results.
