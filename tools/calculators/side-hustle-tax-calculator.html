Federal income tax is separate

Self-employment tax is only one part of what you may owe. Federal income tax is separate, and it depends on your total income from all sources and your filing status. It is not included in the estimate above.

For tax year 2026, the standard deduction is $16,100 for single filers and $32,200 for married couples filing jointly, and the federal marginal rates are 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35%, and 37%. The 37% rate starts above $640,600 of taxable income for single filers and above $768,700 for married couples filing jointly. Source: IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32, announced in IR-2025-103. Figures for 2025 differ — check the IRS for that year.

When NOT to use Side Hustle Tax Calculator

  • This is an estimate of self-employment tax only — it does not calculate your federal or state income tax, which depend on your total income.
  • It does not handle the extra 0.9% Medicare tax in full (Form 8959) — it only flags when your income may pass the threshold.
  • It assumes all of your side-hustle income is self-employment income; rental, investment, and other income follow different rules.

Questions people ask

What is self-employment tax?

Self-employment tax is the Social Security and Medicare tax paid by people who work for themselves. For 2026 it is 15.3% in total — 12.4% Social Security plus 2.9% Medicare — applied to 92.35% of your net self-employment earnings. Employees pay half through payroll and their employer pays the other half; self-employed people pay both halves.

Do I owe self-employment tax if my side hustle is small?

Yes, if your net earnings from self-employment are $400 or more for the year, you owe self-employment tax. Below $400, no self-employment tax is due — though income tax rules still apply.

Is there a cap on self-employment tax?

The 12.4% Social Security part stops at the wage base: $184,500 for 2026 ($176,100 for 2025). The 2.9% Medicare part has no cap. An extra 0.9% Medicare tax can apply when self-employment income plus Medicare wages pass $200,000 single or $250,000 married filing jointly.

Can I deduct any of the self-employment tax?

One-half of your self-employment tax is deductible from your income tax. Federal income tax itself is separate from self-employment tax and depends on your total income and filing status — see the 2026 standard deduction and bracket figures in the note below.
