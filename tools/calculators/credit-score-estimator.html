How to improve each factor

  • Payment history: Pay every bill on time — set up autopay for at least the minimum payment.
  • Utilization: Pay card balances down below 30% of your limits, and under 10% if you can.
  • Length of history: Keep your oldest card open, even if you rarely use it — closing it shortens your history.
  • New credit: Apply for new credit only when you need it — each hard inquiry can lower your score for a while.
  • Credit mix: Do not open accounts just for variety — a mix helps a little, but on-time payments and low balances matter far more.

When NOT to use Credit Score Estimator

Questions people ask

Is this my real credit score?

It is not. This tool has no connection to Equifax, Experian or TransUnion and cannot see your credit file. It gives an educational range based on the answers you select, using a simple model that approximates FICO weights. Check your real score free at AnnualCreditReport.com.

How is the estimate calculated?

The model follows the published FICO weights: payment history 35%, amounts owed and credit utilization 30%, length of credit history 15%, new credit 10%, and credit mix 10%. Each answer is scored and mapped to the 300-850 scale, then shown as a range, never a single exact number.

What improves a credit score the most?

Paying on time and keeping credit card balances low relative to your limits are the two biggest factors — together they are 65% of the model. Paying down high balances usually helps the most, fastest.

Why does it show a range instead of one number?

A range is honest. A real score depends on details in your credit file that a short questionnaire cannot see, so any tool that gives one exact number from five questions is pretending. This tool always shows a range, such as about 670-710.
