About the math: gross revenue needed equals (target income plus expenses) divided by (1 minus the tax set-aside rate). The hourly rate is that gross revenue divided by billable hours per year. The 80 percent rate uses 80 percent of those hours.

When NOT to use Freelance Hourly Rate Calculator

  • The tax set-aside is a planning percentage you choose, not a calculated tax bill. Real tax depends on your full situation.
  • It assumes every billable hour is paid at the same rate, with no unpaid invoices or gaps between clients.
  • The result is a starting point for pricing, not a promise of income.

Questions people ask

How is the hourly rate worked out?

Add your target income and your annual business expenses, then divide by the share left after your tax set-aside. That gives the gross revenue you need. Divide that by your billable hours for the year (hours per week times weeks per year) to get the hourly rate.

Why is the rate higher than my target income divided by my hours?

Because expenses and taxes come out of what you bill. A freelancer who wants $80,000 in the bank still has to bill enough to cover software, equipment, and taxes on top of that $80,000.

What is the 80 percent billable rate?

Not every working hour is billable. Admin, pitching, and learning take time too. The 80 percent rate shows what to charge if only 80 percent of your hours are paid, so unpaid time does not eat your income.

Does the tax set-aside cover my real tax bill?

No. It is a planning percentage you choose. Real US freelancer tax depends on your total income, deductions, state, and self-employment tax, so check the current rules or a tax professional before relying on it.
