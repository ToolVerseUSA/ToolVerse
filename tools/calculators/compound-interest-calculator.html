About the math: interest compounds monthly at the annual rate divided by 12, with your contribution added each month. The same method is used in the high-yield savings calculator. Real rates change, and investment returns can fall as well as rise.

When NOT to use Compound Interest Calculator

  • It assumes the same interest rate and the same monthly contribution for the whole period. Real returns change.
  • It does not account for taxes on interest or gains, or for fees.
  • The result is a projection, not a guarantee of future returns.

Questions people ask

How does compound interest work here?

Interest is calculated monthly on the current balance, then added to it, so the next month earns interest on a slightly larger balance. Your monthly contribution is added each month. The rate you enter is assumed to stay the same for the whole period.

What is the difference between total contributed and total interest?

Total contributed is your starting amount plus every monthly contribution. Total interest is the final balance minus total contributed, so it is the growth earned on top of what you put in.

Will my money really grow by this much?

Only if the rate you enter stays the same for the whole period. Real savings and investment returns change, and investment returns can be negative, so treat this as a projection, not a promise.

Does this include taxes or fees?

No. Taxes on interest or investment gains and any account fees are not included, so the real after-tax result will differ.
