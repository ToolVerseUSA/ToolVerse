About the math: regular pay is your hourly rate times your regular hours. Overtime pay is 1.5 times your hourly rate times your overtime hours. Monthly pay is weekly pay times 52 divided by 12, and annual pay is weekly pay times 52.

The federal rule: under the Fair Labor Standards Act, covered, non-exempt employees must be paid at least 1.5 times their regular rate of pay for every hour over 40 in a workweek. A workweek is a fixed, recurring 168-hour period. Federal law does not require premium pay for weekend or holiday work by itself, only for hours over 40 in the week.

Exempt employees: salaried white-collar employees who are exempt from overtime are not covered by the 1.5 times rule. In 2026 the salary threshold for the white-collar exemption is $684 per week ($35,568 per year). A 2024 attempt to raise that threshold was vacated by a federal court in November 2024.

State rules: some states add daily overtime rules. California Labor Code section 510 requires overtime at 1.5 times after 8 hours in a day and double time after 12 hours in a day. Alaska and Nevada have daily rules too. This tool uses the federal weekly rule only.

Worked example: at $20 per hour with 40 regular hours and 10 overtime hours, the overtime rate is $30, regular pay is $800, overtime pay is $300, total pay is $1,100 per week, $4,766.67 per month, and $57,200 per year.

When NOT to use Overtime Pay Calculator

  • It uses the federal weekly rule only. It does not apply state daily overtime rules, such as California's rule after 8 hours in a day.
  • It does not decide whether you are exempt from overtime. Exempt status depends on your job duties and salary, not just your pay rate.
  • It does not include taxes, deductions, tips, or bonuses, so take-home pay will be lower.

Questions people ask

What is the federal overtime rule?

Under the Fair Labor Standards Act, covered, non-exempt employees must be paid at least 1.5 times their regular rate of pay for every hour over 40 in a workweek. A workweek is a fixed, recurring 168-hour period. Source: Fair Labor Standards Act, 29 U.S.C. section 207, enforced by the U.S. Department of Labor Wage and Hour Division (DOL Fact Sheet 23).

Do I get overtime for working weekends or holidays?

No. Federal law requires premium pay only for hours over 40 in a workweek, not for weekend or holiday work by itself. Some states add daily overtime rules. This tool uses the federal weekly rule only.

Does overtime apply to salaried employees?

Salaried white-collar employees who are exempt from overtime are not covered by the 1.5 times rule. In 2026 the salary threshold for the white-collar exemption is $684 per week ($35,568 per year). A 2024 attempt to raise that threshold was vacated by a federal court in November 2024.

Do any states have daily overtime rules?

Yes. California Labor Code section 510 requires overtime at 1.5 times after 8 hours in a day and double time after 12 hours in a day. Alaska and Nevada have daily rules too. This tool uses the federal weekly rule only, so California workers should check the daily rule as well.

Sources
