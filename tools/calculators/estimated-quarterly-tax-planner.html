Worked example: a single filer with $80,000 of net self-employment income and no other income has a self-employment tax base of $73,880 (92.35% of $80,000). Social Security tax is $9,161 (12.4%) and Medicare tax is $2,143 (2.9%), for $11,304 of self-employment tax. Half of that, $5,652, is deductible. Taxable income is $80,000 minus $5,652 minus the $16,100 standard deduction, which is $58,248. Income tax on that is about $7,527, so total annual tax is about $18,830 and each quarterly payment is about $4,708.

Do you have to pay quarterly? You generally must pay estimated tax if you expect to owe $1,000 or more for the year. The safe harbor rule lets you avoid an underpayment penalty if you pay at least 90% of this year's tax, or 100% of last year's tax (110% if last year's adjusted gross income was over $150,000). Source: IRS Form 1040-ES.

About the math: self-employment tax is 15.3% in total, 12.4% Social Security on the first $184,500 plus 2.9% Medicare with no cap, applied to 92.35% of net self-employment earnings. It applies when net earnings reach $400. Taxable income is net self-employment income plus other income, minus half of the self-employment tax, minus the 2026 standard deduction for your filing status, then taxed with the 2026 federal brackets.

What this does not include: the extra 0.9% Medicare tax above $200,000 single or $250,000 married filing jointly, and state income tax.

Questions people ask

Do I have to pay estimated tax quarterly?

If you expect to owe $1,000 or more in tax for the year, you generally must pay estimated tax in quarterly installments. The four due dates for tax year 2026 are April 15, 2026, June 15, 2026, September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027. Source: IRS Form 1040-ES.

What is self-employment tax?

Self-employment tax is the Social Security and Medicare tax paid by people who work for themselves. For 2026 it is 15.3% in total, 12.4% Social Security on the first $184,500 plus 2.9% Medicare with no cap, applied to 92.35% of your net self-employment earnings. Half of the self-employment tax is deductible from your income tax. Source: IRS Schedule SE. The Social Security wage base of $184,500 for 2026 was set by the Social Security Administration.

What is the safe harbor rule?

The safe harbor rule lets you avoid an underpayment penalty if you pay at least 90% of this year's tax, or 100% of last year's tax. The 100% figure rises to 110% if last year's adjusted gross income was over $150,000. Source: IRS Form 1040-ES.

Does this planner include my other income?

Yes. This planner adds your other income to your net self-employment income, subtracts half of your self-employment tax and the 2026 standard deduction, then taxes the result with the 2026 federal brackets from IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32. It does not include state tax or the extra 0.9% Medicare tax, which can apply above $200,000 single or $250,000 married filing jointly.

Is there a cap on self-employment tax?

No. For 2026, the Social Security wage base is $184,500, so the 12.4% part stops there. The 2.9% Medicare part has no cap. The 2027 wage base is $192,300, according to the Social Security Administration, but this planner uses 2026 figures only.
