What this comparison estimates

The calculator annualizes salary or hourly pay, subtracts simplified federal, payroll, and state planning estimates, then subtracts rent and the recurring costs you entered. It shows the remaining estimated monthly cash for each scenario and the difference between them.

What can change the real result

Actual tax liability can differ because of local taxes, credits, dependents, deductions, residency, benefit elections, bonuses, equity, variable hours, and withholding choices. A job decision can also involve growth, flexibility, family, commute quality, health coverage, and other non-cash factors that no calculator can weigh for you.

Methodology and limitations

ToolVerse uses the shared 2026-planning-v1 method, last reviewed August 22, 2026. Federal tax is modeled with progressive planning brackets and standard deductions by filing status. Employee Social Security and Medicare are included; state income tax is modeled with simplified state planning rates. The tool does not model local income taxes, actual W-4 withholding, credits, pretax retirement contributions, or employer-specific benefit rules.

Questions people ask

Should I compare job offers using gross salary or take-home pay?

Gross salary is a useful starting point, but take-home pay and recurring costs can make two offers feel very different month to month. This calculator estimates both scenarios from the inputs you provide.

Why does the state change a job-offer comparison?

State income-tax rules and local circumstances can change estimated take-home pay. ToolVerse uses simplified state planning rates and does not calculate local taxes or an exact tax return.

Does this calculator tell me which job to accept?

No. It shows the estimated monthly cash difference under the information you enter. Benefits, career goals, work conditions, family needs, local taxes, and other personal factors can matter too.
