What cost of living by state can show

State-level information can help you frame a comparison, especially when a move or job change is under consideration. The Bureau of Economic Analysis publishes Regional Price Parities, which measure differences in price levels across states and metropolitan areas for a given year relative to the national level.[1] The Census Bureau publishes American Community Survey rent tables with a defined geography, survey year, housing universe, and margin of error.[2]

Price context is not a household budget

A price index does not know your rent, commute, childcare, health costs, debt, benefits, household size, or savings target. Use it to ask better questions, then enter your own values into the calculator.

Median rent is not a rent recommendation

ACS median gross rent is a population statistic for a specified geography and renter-occupied units paying cash rent. It is not a suggested lease price or proof that a home is affordable for a particular household.

Data vintage, sources, and limitations

Any external statistic added to this hub in the future should display the source, table or release, geography, year, and relevant limitation. Do not use an old figure as if it were current, and do not publish unsupported “cheapest” or “best” state rankings.

Frequently asked questions

What does cost of living by state measure?
It can describe broad price-level or rent context for a defined period and geography. It does not determine a particular household’s actual budget.
Is statewide cost of living the same as rent affordability?
No. Personal affordability depends on income, taxes, debt, expenses, savings needs, and location within the state.
Why can cities in the same state differ?
Statewide measures can hide meaningful metro, county, and neighborhood differences. Review the exact geography and data vintage before comparing figures.
Does ToolVerse include state taxes?
The main calculator uses simplified planning rates for all states and Washington, D.C. It does not model every deduction, credit, local tax, or withholding rule.
