Emergency Fund Calculator: Your Real Number

“Save 3 to 6 months” is only half the advice. Your real target depends on your essential expenses, how many incomes you rely on, and how steady they are — calculate yours in under a minute.

Planning information only. Targets and timelines are planning estimates based on the expenses and situation you enter — not financial advice, tax advice, or a guarantee. Your actual cushion needs depend on your full circumstances, including insurance and job market conditions.

Find your real emergency fund number

Generic advice says “3 to 6 months” — your target depends on how many incomes you rely on and how steady they are.

Why “months of expenses” beats a flat dollar amount

A $10,000 fund is generous for a household spending $2,000 a month and thin for one spending $5,000. Months of essential expenses scales the target to your real life: it answers “how long could we cover the basics if income stopped tomorrow?” — which is the only question an emergency fund really has to answer.

Build it in layers

You do not need the full target before it starts helping. A first layer of $1,000 covers the most common surprises (a car repair, a copay). The next layer — one month of essentials — turns a job gap from a crisis into an inconvenience. After that, build toward your full 3, 6, or 9-month number at whatever monthly pace your budget allows.

SituationCommon targetReason
Two stable incomes3 monthsBoth incomes rarely stop at once
One stable income6 monthsA single job loss removes all income
Variable income or dependents9 monthsIncome swings and more people to cover

Frequently asked questions

Where should I keep my emergency fund?
In a separate, easy-to-reach savings account — many people use a high-yield savings account (HYSA) so the money earns some interest while staying available within a few days. Avoid investing it: the fund's job is to be there on your worst week, not to grow.
Is 3 months enough, or do I need 6?
Three months is a sensible core target when two stable incomes support the household. Six months fits most single-income households better, and variable or freelance income — or dependents — can justify nine. This calculator applies exactly that logic to your answers.
What counts as an emergency expense?
Essentials only: housing, utilities, groceries, insurance premiums, basic transport, and minimum debt payments. Subscriptions, dining out, and upgrades are paused in a real emergency, so they do not need fund coverage.
Should I build the fund before paying off debt?
A small starter fund (around $1,000 or one month of essentials) usually comes first, so a surprise does not force new borrowing. After that, many people split extra money between high-APR debt and the fund until the fund reaches its full target.
